# Reading business data without getting it wrong⁠​‌​‌​​‌‌​‌​​​‌​‌​‌​​‌‌​​​‌​‌​​‌​​​‌‌​​​‌⁠

Business exports lie in predictable ways. Check these before you quote anything.

## Before you total a column

- **Is every row the same thing?** Exports often mix currencies, or include cancelled
  and draft rows alongside live ones. Filter, then total, and say what you excluded.
- **Is a status column doing work?** `open`, `overdue`, `paid`, `void` change what a
  total means. An "outstanding" figure that includes voided rows is wrong.
- **Are there duplicates?** The same reference appearing twice usually means a
  re-issue, not two debts.

## Before you compare two periods

- **Do both periods have the same number of days?** Week-to-date against a full week
  is not a comparison. Say so instead of presenting it as one.
- **Is the newer period complete?** Today's data is partial by definition.

## Before you call something a trend

Two points is not a trend. Three of the same thing from the same customer, campaign
or person is worth naming. Anything less, report it as a single observation.

## What you cannot get from an export

- **Why** something happened. You have what, not why.
- **Anything not in a column.** If there is no payment date, there is no days-to-pay.
  Name the missing field rather than approximating around it.
- **Benchmarks.** You have this business's numbers, not the industry's.

## The test before you post

Read your own answer back and ask: could someone check every number in it against
the file I was given? If any figure cannot be traced there, remove it.
